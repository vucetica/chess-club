Lesson 4

Basic checkmates

Winning pieces is not enough. You have to finish with a checkmate. These are the mating patterns you will use in your games. Press Next to walk through each one.

The back-rank mate

This is a rather common checkmate. A castled king hides behind three pawns, and one day those three pawns become a wall it cannot climb.

A habit that can save your king. When the position quietens down, play h3 (or h6 for Black). That pawn move gives your king an escape square and makes back-rank mate less likely.

The ladder: two rooks

Two rooks can mate a lone king without any help at all. They take one rank away at a time, like climbing a ladder, and they always stay far away from the enemy king so they can never be captured.

King and queen against a lone king

You will promote a pawn one day and need to know this. The queen shrinks the box, and then the king walks up to help. A queen can never mate on her own.

Watch out for stalemate. With a queen against a lone king, keep the queen a knight's move away from the enemy king and always check that your opponent still has a legal move. See Special Rules for what happens when they do not.

King and rook against a lone king

Harder than the queen, and worth practising with a friend. The rook cuts the board in half, and the king pushes the enemy king backwards using the opposition.

Scholar's Mate, and how to stop it

Somebody at club will try this on you. It works exactly once.

Smothered mate

The prettiest checkmate there is: the king is surrounded by its own pieces, and a lone knight delivers the blow from a undefended square.

And here is the full version, known as Philidor's Legacy. White gives up the queen on purpose so that Black's own rook blocks the last escape square.

Epaulette mate

A reminder that having lots of pieces is not the same as being safe. Here the king is trapped by its own two rooks, standing like shoulder pads on either side.

Ready to try them yourself? The puzzles page has six checkmate-in-one positions and four checkmate-in-two positions to solve on a real board.
