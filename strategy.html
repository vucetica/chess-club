Lesson 6

Strategy and tactics

Tactics are short tricks that win something right now. Strategy is the slow plan that makes those tricks appear. Beginners win games with tactics, so we start there.

Before every single move

Ask these three questions:

  1. Why did my opponent play that move? What are they attacking?
  2. What is not defended? Check every piece of theirs, and every piece of yours.
  3. Do I have a check, a capture or a threat? Look at those first.

The fork

One piece, two targets. Your opponent can only save one of them. Knights are the best forkers in chess because their moves are the hardest to see coming.

The pin

A pin freezes a piece by putting something more valuable behind it. Then you attack the frozen piece again and again, because it cannot run away.

When the piece behind is the king, the pinned piece may not move at all. That is an absolute pin. Click the black knight below and see for yourself.

The skewer

A pin turned back to front. The valuable piece is in front, it has to move, and you capture whatever was hiding behind it.

The discovered attack

One piece moves out of the way and uncovers an attack from the piece behind it, while making a threat of its own. Two threats, one move, and your opponent can only answer one.

Double check

The most forcing move in all of chess. Two pieces give check at once, so blocking and capturing are both useless: no single move can stop two checks. The king must move.

Removing the defender

Before you accept that a square is protected, ask who is protecting it, and whether you can take that piece, chase it away, or make it do two jobs at once.

Now for the strategy

Control the centre

The four squares d4, e4, d5 and e5 are the high ground. A knight on e5 attacks eight squares; the same knight on a1 attacks two. Pieces in the centre are simply worth more.

Keep your pieces active

Count how many squares each of your pieces can reach. If a bishop is staring at its own pawn, it is not really playing. Good players spend whole moves improving their worst piece.

Look after your king

Castle early. Do not push the pawns in front of your castled king without a reason, because each one you push leaves a hole that a queen or bishop can crawl into.

Pawns cannot go backwards

Every pawn move is permanent, so think twice before making one. Three shapes to know:

ShapeWhat it isGood or bad?
Doubled pawnsTwo of your pawns on the same fileUsually a small weakness, because they cannot defend each other
Isolated pawnNo friendly pawn on either side of itNeeds pieces to babysit it, but it does control good squares
Passed pawnNo enemy pawn can stop it reaching the endExcellent, and it wins endgames

Trade when you are ahead

If you are a rook up, swap pieces at every fair opportunity. Each trade makes your extra rook a bigger share of what is left on the board. If you are behind, do the opposite: keep pieces on and keep the position complicated.
