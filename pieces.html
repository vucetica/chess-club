Lesson 1

How the pieces move

Every board below is live: click a piece and its legal squares light up.

The set-up

Chess is played by two people. One plays the white pieces, the other plays the black pieces (they are called white and black pieces even if they are a different color), and both armies are exactly the same size: eight pawns, two rooks, two knights, two bishops, a queen and a king.

Turn rule. White always moves first. Then Black moves, then White, then Black, all the way to the end of the game. You may never move twice in a row and you may never move your opponent's pieces. You also cannot pass: when it is your turn, you have to move something.

Your job is to trap the other player's king. That is called checkmate, and Lesson 4 shows you how to do it.

First, the board

A chessboard has 64 squares. The columns going up and down are called files and they are named a to h. The rows going across are called ranks and they are numbered 1 to 8. Put them together and every square has its own name, like e4 or g7.

Set-up rule. Turn the board so each player has a white square in the bottom right corner. Then the white queen starts on a white square and the black queen starts on a black square: queen on her own color.

The pawn

Pawns are the little ones and they move straight ahead but capture diagonally. A pawn may never move backwards.

  • Normally a pawn moves one square forward.
  • On its very first move only, a pawn may go two squares instead.
  • A pawn captures by moving one square diagonally forward.

The rook

The rook moves in straight lines: up, down, left or right, as far as it likes. It cannot jump over anything.

The bishop

The bishop moves diagonally, as far as it likes. Here is the consequence of that diagonal movement: a bishop that starts on a white square stays on white squares for the entire game.

The knight

The knight moves in an L shape: two squares one way, then one square to the side. It is the only piece that can jump over other pieces.

Easy way to remember. A knight always lands on a square of the opposite color to the one it started on. If you cannot see the jump, look for the nearest squares of the other color that are not next door.

The queen

The queen is a rook and a bishop in one piece: straight lines and diagonals, as far as she likes. She is the most powerful piece on the board, which is exactly why you should not throw her into the middle of the enemy army early in the game.

The king

The king moves one square in any direction. He is slow, but he is the whole game: if your king cannot escape from an attack, you lose.

The one rule kings cannot break. A king may never move onto a square where it would be captured. That is why the board below will not let you go everywhere.

What is each piece worth?

Nobody counts points during a real game, but this table tells you whether a trade is a good deal. If you can win a rook for a knight, you are ahead.

  • ♞︎Knight3 points
  • ♝︎Bishop3 points
  • ♜︎Rook5 points
  • ♛︎Queen9 points
